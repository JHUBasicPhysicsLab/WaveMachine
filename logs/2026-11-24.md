## Discoveries

- The applet was made with Easy Java Simulations 3.4. The readable source is
  `original/_library/TorsionalOscillator.xml`: the model is a 12-rod chain,
  `θ̈ᵢ = −k0 θᵢ − b θ̇ᵢ + k(θᵢ₊₁ − 2θᵢ + θᵢ₋₁)`, with the drive torque `τ₀ cos φ` on rod 0 and
  `φ̇ = ω`, `ω̇ = sweep rate`. The original solved it with RKF45 at dt = 0.1. The new version uses
  fixed-step RK4 with h ≤ 0.05.
- The old "freq" is an angular frequency ω (rad/s), matching ω₀ = 0.5 s⁻¹ in the handout.
- Exact normal modes of the discrete chain:
  - both ends free: ω² = ω₀² + 4 sin²(πm/24), m = 0…11
  - right end clamped: ω² = ω₀² + 4 sin²((2m+1)π/46), m = 0…10

## Checks (run headless with macOS JavaScriptCore against `web/wavemachine.js`)

- No drive, no damping: energy drift −3×10⁻⁵ over 500 s.
- Steady-state response, free end: peaks at 0.500, 0.565, 0.720, 0.915, 1.120. Predicted: 0.500,
  0.564, 0.720, 0.914, 1.118.
- Clamped end: peaks at 0.520, 0.645, 0.835, 1.045, 1.255. Predicted: 0.518, 0.645, 0.836, 1.047, 1.257.
- Forbidden band (ω = 0.2): amplitude falls by 0.635 per rod. Predicted: e^(−κ) = 0.635.

## Later: energy units

- Discovery: the original model has no real units. The handout gives I = 1.0 and C = 1.0 without
  units, though it does use seconds (ω₀ = 0.5 s⁻¹). The code works with E/I.
- Decision: read the handout's values as SI (I = 1 kg·m², C = 1 N·m/rad), so energy is in joules
  and torque in N·m. Labels: energy axis "energy (J)"; readouts "energy = … J"; "Drive torque τ₀ (N·m)";
  "Damping b (s⁻¹)". The model section has a new **Units** paragraph that states this assumption, gives
  the energy formula, and notes that a real machine's energies are much smaller.

## Later: explanation of the predicted resonances

- Under the plot's checkboxes, a new link "How are the predicted resonances calculated?" opens a
  collapsible section (`<details id="resonances">`) in the page text. It's collapsed by default so it
  doesn't hand students the answers to the handout's Problem 3, and it opens automatically from the
  link or from a `#resonances` URL.
- The section walks through four steps: try a standing wave, derive the dispersion relation
  ω² = ω₀² + 4s² sin²(q/2), apply the free and clamped boundary conditions, and give the result. It
  includes a table of all mode frequencies (free, the continuum approximation from the handout, and
  clamped) and lists why measured peaks differ (sweep lag, damping, drive location). It also points
  out the upper band edge, √(ω₀² + 4s²) ≈ 2.06 rad/s.
- Table values computed with Python; they match `predictedModes()` in `wavemachine.js` and the
  simulated peaks recorded earlier in this log.
